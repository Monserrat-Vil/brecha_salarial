# 📊 Gender Wage Gap Analysis in Mexico (2010–2025)
## 📌 Project Overview

Despite years of discussions around gender equality, an important question remains:
> **If women's salaries have been increasing over time, why does the gender wage gap still exist?**

This project analyzes the evolution of the gender wage gap in Mexico between **2010 and 2025**, comparing both **nominal** and **real wages** adjusted using the **National Consumer Price Index (INPC)**.

The objective is to determine whether the observed improvements represent a real reduction in inequality or simply reflect salary growth affected by changes in purchasing power.

---

# 🎯 Research Questions
This project aims to answer the following questions:

- Are women closing the wage gap over time?
- Does the absolute wage gap continue growing?
- How does adjusting salaries using the INPC change the interpretation?
- What happened to purchasing power between 2010 and 2025?
- Does the evidence suggest structural persistence in the gender wage gap?

# 📊 Key Findings

The analysis suggests that:

- Both men's and women's salaries increased over time.
- After adjusting salaries using the INPC, purchasing power followed a different trend than nominal wages.
- Women experienced a slightly faster relative salary growth. However, the absolute wage gap remains persistent.
- The Female/Male salary ratio improved only slightly during the analyzed period.
- The evidence suggests a **slow structural convergence** rather than a complete reduction of wage inequality.

---

# 💡 Main Conclusion

Although the gender wage gap shows signs of improvement in relative terms, the convergence is slow.

Adjusting salaries using the INPC reveals that increases in nominal wages do not necessarily translate into equivalent gains in purchasing power.

The results suggest that the gender wage gap persists over time, supporting the hypothesis of **structural persistence** in wage inequality.
